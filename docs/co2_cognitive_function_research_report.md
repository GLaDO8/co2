# CO2 and Cognitive Function: A Research Report

## Executive Summary

This report synthesizes peer-reviewed research on the cognitive effects of elevated carbon dioxide (CO2) concentrations. The evidence demonstrates that CO2 levels commonly found in indoor environments (800-1,400 ppm) can significantly impair cognitive function, particularly higher-order decision-making, strategic thinking, and crisis response. Effects have been documented across diverse populations including office workers, students, submarine crews, and pilots.

---

## 1. Cognitive Decline at Specific CO2 Levels

### 1.1 Key Finding: Linear Dose-Response Relationship

**Landmark Study: Allen et al. (2016)** - *Environmental Health Perspectives*

The most comprehensive controlled study to date found a linear dose-response relationship between CO2 and cognitive function:

| CO2 Level | Cognitive Impact |
|-----------|-----------------|
| 550 ppm (Green+) | Baseline (reference) |
| 945 ppm (Moderate) | **15% decrease** in cognitive scores |
| 1,400 ppm (High) | **50% decrease** in cognitive scores |

**Critical Finding:** Cognitive function scores were 15% lower at ~945 ppm and 50% lower at ~1,400 ppm compared to 550 ppm. For every 400 ppm increase in CO2, cognitive scores decreased by approximately **21%** after adjusting for participant variability.

**Citation:**
> Allen JG, MacNaughton P, Satish U, Santanam S, Vallarino J, Spengler JD. Associations of Cognitive Function Scores with Carbon Dioxide, Ventilation, and Volatile Organic Compound Exposures in Office Workers: A Controlled Exposure Study of Green and Conventional Office Environments. *Environ Health Perspect*. 2016;124(6):805-812. doi:10.1289/ehp.1510037

### 1.2 Higher-Order Cognitive Domains Most Affected

The Allen et al. (2016) study tested nine cognitive domains using the Strategic Management Simulation (SMS) tool. The largest impairments were observed in:

- **Crisis Response:** 97% higher scores at Green vs. Conventional; 131% higher at Green+ vs. Conventional
- **Information Usage:** 172% higher at Green vs. Conventional; 299% higher at Green+ vs. Conventional  
- **Strategy:** 183% higher at Green vs. Conventional; 288% higher at Green+ vs. Conventional

**Key Insight:** Higher-order cognitive functions requiring complex decision-making, planning, and strategic thinking show the greatest sensitivity to elevated CO2.

## 2. Special Populations Research

### 2.1 Submarine Crews

**Study: Rodeheffer et al. (2018)** - *Aerospace Medicine and Human Performance*

Submarines routinely operate with CO2 levels of **2,000-5,000 ppm**—significantly higher than typical indoor environments (400-600 ppm). This study tested 36 submarine-qualified sailors:

**Results:**
- No significant differences in decision-making performance at 600, 2,500, or 15,000 ppm
- **Important Caveat:** The authors noted that submariners may have developed physiological adaptation to elevated CO2 through chronic exposure
- The study could not replicate the cognitive deficits found in office workers and students at similar CO2 levels

**Implications:** Chronic exposure may lead to physiological adaptation, but this does not negate the documented effects in non-adapted populations.

**Citation:**
> Rodeheffer CD, Chabal S, Clarke JM, Fothergill DM. Acute Exposure to Low-to-Moderate Carbon Dioxide Levels and Submariner Decision Making. *Aerosp Med Hum Perform*. 2018;89(6):520-525. doi:10.3357/AMHP.5010.2018

### 2.2 Pilots and Aviation

**Study: Grassmann et al. (2016)** - *Ergonomics*

Research on 63 male pilot candidates found that mental load during complex cognitive tasks was associated with:
- Increased respiration rate
- **Decreased end-tidal CO2 (petCO2)**
- Stronger respiratory reactivity correlated with better performance

**Key Finding:** Pilots with better performance showed stronger respiratory reactivity to mental load, suggesting that CO2 regulation may be linked to cognitive performance capacity.

**Citation:**
> Grassmann M, Vlemincx E, von Leupoldt A, Van den Bergh O. The role of respiratory measures to assess mental load in pilot selection. *Ergonomics*. 2016;59(6):745-753. doi:10.1080/00140139.2015.1090019

### 2.3 Astronauts and Space Travel

**Study: Carr et al. (2025)** - *Experimental Physiology* [Review]

Chronic elevations of inspired CO2 in spacecraft have significant cerebral implications:

**Key Mechanisms:**
- Cerebrovascular regulation is critically dependent on arterial CO2 partial pressure (PaCO2)
- CO2 affects cerebral blood flow, tissue PCO2, tissue proton concentration, cerebral metabolism, and cognitive/neuronal function
- Chronic hypercapnia leads to deterioration of cognitive function

**Citation:**
> Carr JMJR, Ainslie PN, Day T. Confined spaces in space: Cerebral implications of chronic elevations of inspired carbon dioxide and implications for long-duration space travel. *Exp Physiol*. 2025. doi:10.1113/EP091659

## 3. Decision-Making Impairment Studies

### 3.1 Strategic Thinking and Crisis Response

The Strategic Management Simulation (SMS) test—used in multiple studies—measures real-world decision-making capabilities. Research consistently shows that elevated CO2 impairs:

1. **Crisis Response:** Ability to plan, stay prepared, and strategize under emergency conditions
2. **Information Usage:** Capacity to use provided and gathered information toward attaining goals
3. **Strategy:** Complex thinking involving well-integrated solutions with optimal use of information and planning
4. **Information Seeking:** Capacity to gather information from available sources

### 3.2 Critical Review Findings

**Study: Du et al. (2020)** - *Indoor Air* [Critical Review]

A comprehensive review of 37 experimental studies found:

**Key Conclusions:**
- Pure CO2 at concentrations common in indoor environments affects **high-level decision-making** measured by SMS in non-specialized populations
- Lower ventilation and accumulation of indoor pollutants (including CO2) can reduce the **speed** of various cognitive functions while leaving accuracy unaffected
- The cognitive impact of CO2 is **inconsistent across studies** due to variations in assessment methods, study designs, and exposure doses

**Citation:**
> Du B, Tandoc MC, Mack ML, Siegel JA. Indoor CO2 concentrations and cognitive function: A critical review. *Indoor Air*. 2020;30(6):1067-1082. doi:10.1111/ina.12706

## 4. Student Performance in Classrooms

### 4.1 Classroom CO2 Levels

**Study: Fedele et al. (2025)** - *Sensors*

Monitoring of Italian university classrooms found:

| Season | Peak CO2 Level |
|--------|---------------|
| Autumn | **2,324 ppm** (significantly exceeding 1,000 ppm threshold) |
| Spring | 953 ppm |

**Key Finding:** Strong positive correlations (>0.70) between CO2 and humidity suggest inadequate air exchange reduces indoor air quality, potentially affecting cognitive performance.

**Citation:**
> Fedele A, Colantoni A, Calabrò G, Scungio M, Rossi S, Taborri J. Measuring CO2 Concentration and Thermal Comfort in Italian University Classrooms: A Seasonal Analysis. *Sensors (Basel)*. 2025;25(7):1970. doi:10.3390/s25071970

### 4.2 Ventilation and Academic Performance

**Study: Laurent et al. (2021)** - *Environmental Research Letters*

A multicountry longitudinal study found associations between acute exposures to indoor CO2 and cognitive function in office workers, with implications for student populations.

**Citation:**
> Laurent JGC, MacNaughton P, Jones E, et al. Associations between Acute Exposures to PM2.5 and Carbon Dioxide Indoors and Cognitive Function in Office Workers: A Multicountry Longitudinal Prospective Observational Study. *Environ Res Lett*. 2021;16(9):094047. doi:10.1088/1748-9326/ac1bd8

## 5. Office Worker Productivity Research

### 5.1 Comprehensive Office Study

**Study: Allen et al. (2016)** - *Environmental Health Perspectives*

24 professional-grade employees (architects, designers, programmers, engineers, managers) were tested across different indoor environmental quality conditions:

**Results:**
- **61% higher** cognitive scores in Green building (low VOCs) vs. Conventional building
- **101% higher** cognitive scores in Green+ building (high ventilation) vs. Conventional building
- Cognitive function scores were significantly better under Green+ conditions for **all nine functional domains**

### 5.2 Office Ventilation Standards

**Study: Felgueiras et al. (2022)** - *Indoor Air* [Systematic Review]

A review of ventilation conditions in urban offices found:
- Mean CO2 concentration: **665 ppm** (generally acceptable)
- High occupancy density and inadequate HVAC operation can cause CO2 to exceed **1,000 ppm**
- Poor indoor environmental quality significantly impacts worker health and productivity

**Citation:**
> Felgueiras F, Mourão Z, Moreira A, Gabriel MF. A systematic review of ventilation conditions and airborne particulate matter levels in urban offices. *Indoor Air*. 2022;32(11):e13148. doi:10.1111/ina.13148

### 5.3 VOC and CO2 Combined Effects

**Key Finding:** Allen et al. (2016) demonstrated that both VOCs and CO2 are **independently associated** with cognitive scores:
- A 500 μg/m³ increase in TVOCs was associated with a **13% decrease** in cognitive scores
- A 400 ppm increase in CO2 was associated with a **21% decrease** in cognitive scores
- A 20 cfm increase in outdoor air per person was associated with an **18% increase** in cognitive scores

## 6. Mechanisms: How CO2 Affects the Brain

### 6.1 Cerebrovascular Mechanisms

**Study: Mutch & Duffin (2021)** - *Canadian Journal of Anaesthesia*

Regional cerebral blood flow (rCBF) shows marked vasoactive changes in response to CO2 alterations. Differential regional cerebral blood flow reactivity to CO2 has significant implications for cognitive function.

**Citation:**
> Mutch WAC, Duffin J. Differential regional cerebral blood flow reactivity to alterations in end-tidal gases in healthy volunteers. *Can J Anaesth*. 2021;68(10):1497-1506. doi:10.1007/s12630-021-02042-x

### 6.2 Cerebral Blood Flow and Cognition

**Study: Ogoh et al. (2014)** - *Physiological Reports*

Changes in cerebral blood flow (CBF) directly affect cognitive function:
- Manipulating CBF using hypercapnic gas affects cognitive performance
- Increased CBF through CO2 inhalation can improve cognitive function
- **Mechanism:** CO2 is a potent vasodilator; increased arterial CO2 increases cerebral blood flow

**Citation:**
> Ogoh S, Tsukamoto H, Hirasawa A, Hasegawa H, Hirose N, Hashimoto T. The effect of changes in cerebral blood flow on cognitive function during exercise. *Physiol Rep*. 2014;2(9):e12163. doi:10.14814/phy2.12163

### 6.3 Chronic Hypercapnia and Cognitive Deterioration

**Study: Burgraff et al. (2018)** - *Journal of Physiology*

Chronic hypercapnia leads to **deterioration of cognitive function**. Research on goats demonstrated integrative ventilatory, cardiovascular, and metabolic physiological responses to chronic CO2 exposure.

**Citation:**
> Burgraff NJ, Neumueller SE, Buchholz K, et al. Ventilatory and integrated physiological responses to chronic hypercapnia in goats. *J Physiol*. 2018;596(22):5343-5363. doi:10.1113/JP276666

### 6.4 CO2, Blood pH, and Acid-Base Balance

**Study: Maniscalco et al. (2022)** - *Indoor Air*

Exposure to CO2 at 20,000 ppm (much higher than typical indoor levels but relevant for understanding mechanisms) affects:
- Acid-base balance
- Physiological responses
- Cognitive performance

**Mechanism:** CO2 dissolves in blood to form carbonic acid, lowering blood pH (respiratory acidosis). Even mild acidosis can affect neuronal function.

**Citation:**
> Maniscalco J, Hoffmeyer F, Monsé C, et al. Physiological responses, self-reported health effects, and cognitive performance during exposure to carbon dioxide at 20 000 ppm. *Indoor Air*. 2022;32(1):e12939. doi:10.1111/ina.12939

### 6.5 EEG and Cerebral Hemodynamics

**Study: Babiloni et al. (2014)** - *Clinical Neurophysiology*

Hypercapnia affects functional coupling of resting-state electroencephalographic (EEG) rhythms and cerebral hemodynamics:
- Both normal elderly and amnestic mild cognitive impairment subjects showed altered EEG coherence during CO2 exposure
- Changes in cerebrovascular reactivity are linked to altered neuronal function

**Citation:**
> Babiloni C, Vecchio F, Altavilla R, et al. Hypercapnia affects the functional coupling of resting state electroencephalographic rhythms and cerebral haemodynamics in healthy elderly subjects and in patients with amnestic mild cognitive impairment. *Clin Neurophysiol*. 2014;125(4):685-693. doi:10.1016/j.clinph.2013.10.002

## 8. Key Takeaways

1. **Dose-Response Relationship:** Every 400 ppm increase in CO2 is associated with approximately 21% decrease in cognitive scores

2. **High-Order Functions Most Affected:** Strategic thinking, crisis response, and information usage show the greatest sensitivity to CO2

3. **Adaptation Possible:** Chronic exposure may lead to physiological adaptation (as seen in submariners), but this does not apply to typical indoor environments

4. **Mechanism:** CO2 affects cerebral blood flow, blood pH (respiratory acidosis), and EEG coherence—all of which impact neuronal function

5. **Real-World Impact:** CO2 levels commonly found in poorly ventilated classrooms (2,300+ ppm) and offices (1,000+ ppm) can significantly impair cognitive performance

6. **Ventilation is Critical:** Increasing outdoor air ventilation from 20 cfm/person to 40 cfm/person resulted in 101% improvement in cognitive scores
